On Monday, 10 November 2025, Vid gave an invited talk to the University of Edinburgh's [Computer Science Education Group](https://informatics.ed.ac.uk/computer-science-education-cse-group) about the CHAILD project.

This was early in the project, when we didn't have any empirical findings yet. We discussed how generative AI tools now automate work that used to be considered distinctly human. As their use becomes more widespread, how might we design systems to augment humans without replacing them?

That question is sharpest for children and young people. Childhood is when the ability to make informed decisions — and the capacity to act on them — is still forming. A system that supplies plausible answers, generates believable content, recommends what to do next, or simply decides on your behalf can erode that capacity before it has developed.

From our exploration of relevant literature and media, we gathered a few ways to practice agency.

## Four ways of enacting agency in relation to an AI system

- **Resisting** — refusing to use AI, opting out, or withholding your data.
- **Engaging** — using the AI system in an informed and deliberate way, with good knowledge of how it works.
- **Controlling** — steering what the AI system does and how it behaves, potentially outside of its intended use cases, based on knowledge of how it works.
- **Reshaping** — changing the system itself or creating new ones.

Framing it this way makes the point that agency is not simply "using AI" or "not using AI". Sometimes it might not be possible to avoid AI systems (e.g., when they're in the background, when there's no opt out option, when other people are using them) and resisting might not be an option. And sometimes reshaping is not possible without specialist equipment and skills. But there's always something one can do among these options.

The talk ended where the group could take it further: **you are not powerless** — but how do we get there?

- In what ways do you see AI systems challenge human agency?
- How would you operationalise and measure a concept like agency?

We have some good ideas about how AI systems challenge human agency, but measuring agency is still an open question. We will keep working on this in our future work.

Our thanks to the Computer Science Education group for the invitation and for a useful discussion about what all of this means for students in higher education, not only in schools.
