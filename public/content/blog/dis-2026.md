Zaki represented CHAILD at the ACM Designing Interactive Systems conference in Singapore, 13–17 June, with two contributions to the Provocations and Works in Progress track.

![Zaki standing beside the Towards Evaluating Student Agency poster in the conference exhibition hall](/content/blog/images/dis-2026-zaki-poster.jpg "Zaki with the student agency poster at DIS 2026")

Zaki presented two posters: one on AI-mediated assessment and one on evaluating student agency in AI-mediated learning.

## Should machines get to judge?

The first piece, [*Should Machines Get to Judge? Rethinking the Design of AI-Mediated Assessment in Education*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3802974.3809410), co-authored with Manolis, is the more provocative of the two.

![The Should Machines Get to Judge poster, setting out bias, reliability and accountability problems in AI-mediated assessment](/content/blog/images/dis-2026-judge-poster.jpg "The assessment poster")

Handing evaluative authority to a language model results in a redistribution of power over learners, and the known problems — bias, opacity, reliability gaps, accountability voids — do not go away because the throughput improves. The position we argue is that if AI is involved in assessment at all, it belongs in a frame of ongoing human–AI negotiation, not fully automated decision-making.

One of the conference participants who joined to see the poster, posed an interesting challenge: they pushed on what "human oversight" actually means when the human is a time-poor teacher rubber-stamping a queue of AI judgments. Oversight that is structurally impossible is not oversight. We'll aim to cover this in a future publication.

## Evaluating agency, not assuming it

The second poster, [*Towards Evaluating Student Agency in AI-Mediated Learning*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3802974.3809419), co-authored with Vid, Leslye and Manolis, sits closer to the CHAILD project's core: an attempt to make student agency into something designers can actually evaluate.

We frame agency as interactional and multidimensional — influenced by AI system affordances, school decision policies, feedback structures and class orchestration. Alongside that we sketch a methods suite: log-data probes, scenario-based interface walkthroughs, and mixed-methods classroom studies, for spotting when a system narrows or widens a student's capacity to act.

The question that kept coming back at the board was some version of *how do you know agency when you see it in the logs?* It is the right question, and one we are trying to figure out in the longitudinal study we are designing now.

## Two papers that changed our minds

*Choose Your Own Perspective: Designing Role-Based Agency in Heritage Learning*, by Jeffrey Valino Koh and colleagues, was run in four Singapore secondary history classrooms. In history education you cannot let a student rewrite what happened; the epistemic authority has to hold. So where are the opportunities for children to have agency? Their answer is to move it out of outcomes and into interpretation — perspective-based agency. The student cannot change the ending, but they can inhabit a role, read the archive differently, argue with it.

That dismantled an assumption worth naming: that agency means being able to change what the system does. It does not have to. Our own multidimensional framing has probably been underweighting the interpretive dimension.

*From Planning to Revision: How AI Writing Support at Different Stages Alters Ownership* — a between-subjects study of 253 people writing short essays — is a completely different study and the finding is deceptively simple. Any AI help lowers your sense of ownership over the writing. But *where* it helps changes the size of the effect: support at the planning stage barely affects sense of ownership, while support at the drafting stage lowers it significantly. Ownership drops in proportion to how much of the actual text and ideas came from the system, and an AI-drafted essay written from your own outline smuggles in far more of the generated ideas than one might expect.

Ownership is not the same thing as agency, but they are closely related, and this is the cleanest demonstration we have seen that *when* you intervene matters as much as whether you do.
