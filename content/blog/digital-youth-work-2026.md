On Tuesday 10 February — Safer Internet Day — Vid attended [YouthLink Scotland's Digital Youth Work Conference](https://www.youthlink.scot/digital-youth-work-conference-2026-programme/) at the Heart of Midlothian FC Innovation Centre in Edinburgh.

![Delegates seated at round tables watching a presentation, seen from the back of the room](/content/blog/images/dyw-2026-session.jpg "A session at the Digital Youth Work Conference")

The headline was the launch of **Our Futures and AI: A Youth Work Toolkit**, presented by Ryan McKay of the Citadel Youth Centre and Les Ellis of Cromar Future Group. It is full of activities and guidance for exploring AI with young people — and, importantly, it was co-created with them. Ben MacPherson, Minister for Higher and Further Education, welcomed the toolkit and spoke with the young people who made it. We have [added it to our resources page](/resources/).

![Small modelling clay figures on a table, one spelling out CFG](/content/blog/images/dyw-2026-ai-show-models.jpg "Models made during one of the activities from the Youth Work Toolkit")

Dr Alicja Pawluczuk and Cristina Bacalso of the Include Plus Network gave a keynote on *Digital Youth Work as Critical Infrastructure for Digital Inclusion in the UK*, arguing that the sector needs to be far more visible in policy agendas that are increasingly about digitalisation. Alex Hutchison of ForrData followed on *Ethical by Design: CoCreating What "Good" Looks Like in Tech Partnerships* — how youth work and the tech sector might build relationships that are robust and ethical rather than extractive.

There were panels on the future of digital youth work, with Liz Green and Lotte Vermeire, and on social media's role in youth work, chaired by Hilary Phillips.

The day closed with *The Unbelievably Creative AI Show*, an interactive performance from Digital Skills Education about art and AI, where participants have to tell apart AI-assisted artist-created artwork from non-AI-assisted art. We discussed the question of whether AI-assisted art is still art. The room was divided on this. The artist who had used AI tools to create their art said she felt that she was still being creative, because she had to direct the tool with guidance from her own knowledge, try a few different prompts, and refine the outputs.

## Why we were there

Youth work has been doing something we care about for a long time: working *with* young people rather than designing *for* them. The toolkit is a good example — the young people who will use it helped build it. We plan to involve young people in a similar way in our upcoming co-design studies.

It was also a reminder that the people who spend the most time with young people online are often not in the rooms where AI policy gets written. The keynote's argument — that digital youth work should be treated as infrastructure — applies just as much to the research side. If possible, we hope to keep collaborating with youth work groups in our future studies.
