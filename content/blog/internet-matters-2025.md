Jun Zhao was one of four experts who shaped the recommendations in [*Me, Myself and AI*](https://www.internetmatters.org/hub/research/me-myself-and-ai-chatbot-research/), published by Internet Matters on 19 July, on how children are using AI chatbots and what that means for their safety.

The survey behind it found that 54% of children now use generative AI for schoolwork while only 40% of schools discuss it with them. Around a quarter go to chatbots for advice, and more than a third say it feels like talking to a friend — rising to half among vulnerable children.

The report groups the risks into three: over-reliance and emotional attachment, exposure to inaccurate guidance, and a degree of trust that sits awkwardly with how blurred the line has become between a chatbot and a friend.

The recommendations reach for agency rather than restriction. They ask for interactions a parent or carer can share, prompts that invite a child to stop and reflect, deliberate pauses in use, sources a child can trace, and design that asks questions rather than only answering them.

The Oxford Child-Centred AI group has [a fuller write-up](https://oxfordccai.org/post/20-25-07-internet-matters/).
