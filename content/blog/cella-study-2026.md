We are recruiting UK secondary schools for an international study of how students learn with help from generative AI tools.

![Invitation to collaborate on an international study of AI-supported learning in secondary education, with the CELLA and Jacobs Foundation logos alongside those of UCL Knowledge Lab, Radboud University, University of Oulu, Technical University of Munich, Monash University and the University of Central Florida](/content/blog/images/cella-invitation-2026.png)

## The question

When generative AI tools are available, how do students aged 12 to 15 engage in self-regulated learning, i.e., plan, monitor and evaluate their own reading and writing — and what kinds of support help them learn more, rather than simply finish faster?

Generative AI tools can improve how a piece of work looks by the deadline. What is far less clear is what it does to the learning underneath: the planning, the monitoring, the noticing that something is not working and changing course. Those habits are what carry a student into the next task without the tool. The study is looking for the line between a short-term gain in performance and a durable gain in learning, and for the kinds of scaffolding that keep students on the right side of it.

## Who is running it

The study is part of [CELLA](https://www.cellaresearch.org/), the Centre for Learning and Living with AI — a global research centre funded by the Jacobs Foundation and led by Monash University. We are running the UK arm in partnership with Monash. It is now running across more than 25 countries and regions.

## What taking part involves

A single 90-minute in-class session. Students work through a structured reading and writing task in which they propose a renewable energy strategy for a community, drawing on its local marine, wind and forest resources.

The session opens with a short assessment and a brief introduction to the platform, gives students 45 minutes on the reading and writing task, and closes with a second assessment. Students are randomly assigned to one of three kinds of support: a chatbot that answers questions but will not write the essay for them, automatic suggestions prompting them to plan and check their own progress, or both together.

All a school needs is access to laptops or desktops with an internet connection. We provide guidance and support for running the session, and we come to you — the session runs in class, in the students' own setting, to keep disruption to a minimum.
