## Paper highlights

1. [Training Wheels, Not Guardrails: Supporting Teen Agency Through Real-Time Online Safety Nudges for Social Media](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3773077.3806123) by Zainab Agha et al.


We are both interested in the wizard-of-oz methodology being applied to assess the use of nudge to keep teens safe online and the key finding, which shows that when nudges are designed to facilitate teens' autonomy, support their risk awareness, and offer advices, they are much more effective.

2. ["I Want It to Sound Like Me": Understanding Teenagers' Views of Generative AI-Mediated Communication in Friendship](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3773077.3806122) by Seraphina Yong et al.

This is such a fascinating paper that shows how teens' views of genAI can be informed by broader perceptions of AI and their social principles, and how individuals' values for friendships may impact on their perceived effort in the use of AI, and how their perceived degrees of acceptable AI use are based on what is viewed as human.

This is very much consistent with our hypothesis that teen's social values and social relationships with their friends, communities could have a close impact on their agency of interacting with AI.


Thus, it was not surprising to see the authors highlight the importance of considering teens' social confidence and trust in AI in future policy and design developments.

3. [A seat at the table: Teen experiences and perceptions of social media recommendation algorithms](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3773077.3806115) by Megan Nyhan et al.

This piece of amazing research aims to invite teens to the policy table discussing the impact of the legislations like the EU Digital Services Act on teens' experiences with social media platforms.

The scenario-based vignettes method shows that teens have a high awareness of the algorithmic impact on their use of social media while they enjoy using social media to have fun, get inspirations. However, they also report harms, such as the unwanted sexual attention, particularly with late night feeds. While teens are keen to take actions, reporting mechanism are highly ineffective and they demand platform changes.

The findings consistently highlight how we need to move beyond banning teens’ access to social media when they play such an important role in their social life. These powerful voices demand fundamental changes by the large platforms, which banning access alone cannot achieve.


This youth-focus design is also highlighted by the WiP presented by Kim et al - [Social Understanding, Placeness, and Identity Alignment: A Design Framework for Friendship-Supportive Youth Social Media](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3773077.3812190).


4. There are a few really interesting papers from the generative AI sessions, including
- [Anthropomorphism in Children’s Interactions with LLM-Based Chatbots: A Systematic Review of Drivers and Outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1145/3773077.3806126) by Jayathilake and Ma, which highlights succinctly  the drivers, the developmental determinants, and outcomes for children to engage with LLM-based chatbots.
- [Exploring AI Companions Together: A Structured Family Co-Investigation of Character.AI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/epdf/10.1145/3773077.3806139) by Gupta et al, which innovatively brings families together to co-engage with AI companions. While the study involves complicated ethical implications, it provides crucial inputs for us to think about how to involve families and design for families' involvement when safeguarding teens' interactions with AI companions.
