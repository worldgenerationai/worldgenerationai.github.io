On 15 July, Vid led an online session for the [Computing at School](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/) AI community: *AI and Agency: Helping Young People Think for Themselves*. CAS have written [their own account of the session](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2026/july/ai-and-agency-helping-young-people-think-for-themselves-cas-ai-event/), which is worth reading.

The starting point was that AI literacy is not everything. Good use of AI tools is about more than learning to use the tools. We argue that agency — making decisions that are informed, intentional and reflective, rather than relying on decisions the tool has made for you - can also be taught and fostered deliberately.

We looked at what gets in the way: outputs that are consistently biased, over-reliance habits that develop over time, and opaque systems whose reasoning you cannot inspect. Then at what teachers can do about it, which turns out to be less about permitting or banning AI and more about modelling how to use it well. Comparing what different tools say to the same prompt, and checking a factual claim independently before using AI tools, are both small classroom habits that build the reflex of questioning rather than accepting.

That last point echoes what pupils told us at [Beyond the Classroom](/blog/beyond-the-classroom-2026/) in May: they notice when the adults around them use AI freely while restricting it for students. Modelling responsible use is not only good pedagogy — it is the part young people are watching.

CAS run a lively community of computing teachers, and we are always glad of the chance to test our thinking against people who are in classrooms every week. Our earlier notes from [the CAS conference at KCL](/blog/kcl-teachers-conference/) and [the Festival of Computing](/blog/festival-of-computing/) came from the same community and we look forward to continuing the conversations.
