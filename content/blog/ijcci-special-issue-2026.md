We are guest-editing a special issue of the [International Journal of Child-Computer Interaction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/special-issue/331176/agency-in-child-ai-interaction) on agency in child-AI interaction, and the call for papers is now out.

The question behind it is the one this project keeps returning to. Automated systems and synthetic media increasingly shape what people see, do, and decide — generative AI, robots, recommenders, filters, classifiers, predictors — and as their use expands, will we design them to replace human agency or to support it? Systems that produce answers, rankings, or decisions on a user's behalf can erode children's agency. Designed well, they can widen children's scope to explore alternatives, express themselves, learn, collaborate, and take part in decisions that affect them.

The call uses an updated working definition of agency: children and young people's ability to make informed decisions and meaningfully enact them, and their capacity, individually and collectively, to shape their lives and environments. It treats agency both as an ability developed through practice and as a capacity that can be diminished or supported. What the issue asks is how we might study, design, and use AI systems in ways that move beyond keeping humans in the loop towards putting humans in the lead — and whether the agency on offer is real or merely perceived.

## What we are looking for

Full papers of roughly 8,000 to 12,000 words. The themes run from models and theories of agency, through methods for researching and designing for it, interaction design case studies and empirical work, comparative studies across systems, populations or contexts, critical and ethical perspectives, to research tools, policy frameworks, and design principles.

Four things every submission must state explicitly:

1. a working definition of agency;
2. the stakeholder groups involved, which must include children or young people;
3. what the AI system is and what part it plays;
4. how children's voices or perspectives were integrated — through participatory or child-led work, or through the ethical reasoning behind a study that was not participatory.

We expect a good deal of attention to generative AI, but submissions on any data-driven or automated decision-making system are welcome, provided children are part of the interaction.

## Dates and submission

Submissions open on 27 April 2026 and close on **27 October 2026**. Submitted manuscripts will go through the journal's usual peer review process.

The guest editors are Vidminas Vizgirda (University of Oxford and UCL), Isobel Voysey (University of Oxford), Zaki Pauzi (UCL), and Ayça Atabey (University of Edinburgh).

The issue builds on the [CAMCAD workshop](/blog/chi-workshop-2026/) we are running at CHI 2026 in April, but it is deliberately wider: the workshop's theme is collaborative agency, while the special issue is open to agency in all its forms. Our [review of how CCI research has treated agency](/blog/agency-lit-review/) is among the starting points we suggest, alongside work by Bennett et al. on agency and autonomy in HCI, Schaper et al. on computational empowerment, and Iivari et al. on children's transformative agency.
